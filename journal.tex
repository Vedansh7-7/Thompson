\entry{15 Oct 2026: Pivot to experimentation and bandits}{planning}

\jpart{Discussions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DataFlow (earlier project) reads as a backend/engineering project, not data science: no question,
        no model, no inference, synthetic data with circular insights, an aspirational tool list.
  \item Chosen instead: a \textbf{Bayesian experimentation engine} for choosing checkout offers on a
        quick-commerce app. It builds directly on Beta updating, and experimentation is the core skill
        of a Product Analyst.
  \item Deadline: 25 Oct (online assessment on 29 Oct).
\end{itemize}

\jpart{Research (summary)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fixed-horizon A/B tests are still the default; peeking inflates false positives (Evan Miller).
  \item Industry fixes: sequential tests (Spotify: group sequential), always-valid inference (Optimizely),
        variance reduction (CUPED, Microsoft Bing, about 50\%).
  \item Bayesian A/B with expected loss (VWO); Thompson sampling (Chapelle \& Li, Yahoo); bandits in platforms
        (Stitch Fix); contextual bandits (Netflix artwork, Yahoo LinUCB); switchbacks for marketplace
        interference (DoorDash).
  \item Honest framing: bandits are good at \emph{earning while learning}, weaker at \emph{measuring} effects.
        The fair comparison is against a proper A/B test, not a straw man.
\end{itemize}

\entry{18 Oct 2026: Experiment design and the simulator}{design, simulator}

\jpart{Experiment}
Which checkout offer should be shown during a campaign? Unit: one cart-page visit.
Outcome: ordered (0/1) and profit if ordered. Toy values (assumptions, not real data):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clccc}
\toprule
Arm & Offer & True order rate & Profit per order & Profit per visitor \\
\midrule
0 & No offer (control)   & 0.08 & \Rs 90 & \Rs 7.20 \\
1 & Free delivery        & 0.10 & \Rs 70 & \Rs 7.00 \\
2 & \Rs 50 off           & 0.12 & \Rs 40 & \Rs 4.80 \\
3 & \Rs 20 off above 299 & 0.11 & \Rs 70 & \textbf{\Rs 7.70} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Arm 2 converts best but earns least; arm 3 is the true profit winner.

\jpart{Discussions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A/B testing: random split, one change, measure a metric, test whether the gap is noise.
  \item Simulator design critique: faker names unnecessary; truth is one rate per arm; real conversion rates
        are small (1--10\%); returning users must keep the same arm (sticky assignment); unit of analysis
        (user vs visit); simulated clock instead of real waiting; hide the truth from the engine.
  \item Three different things: true rate $p$ (fixed, hidden), outcome (Bernoulli draw), belief about $p$ (Beta posterior).
  \item Arrivals: a non-homogeneous Poisson process. Each minute has its own $\lambda_t$; the daily total
        is Poisson($\sum\lambda_t$), but that says nothing about timing.
  \item Poisson as many unlikely coin flips; spread $=\sqrt{\lambda}$; relative wobble $=1/\sqrt{\lambda}$.
  \item Hashing for assignment: \code{sha256("user\_id:experiment\_id") mod 4}. Plain \code{user\_id \% 4}
        gives identical splits across experiments, so their effects cannot be separated.
  \item Returning users: share of visits from users seen earlier in the window. ``Repeat purchase rate'' is a
        different (customer-level) metric. Chosen: 0.15 as an assumption, with sensitivity runs planned.
\end{itemize}

\jpart{Calculations}
\begin{align*}
\text{relative wobble of a Poisson count} &= \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
  \qquad (\lambda=40:\ 16\%\ \text{per minute};\ \lambda=2400:\ 2\%\ \text{per hour})\\
\text{expected distinct users (pool $M$, $N$ visits)} &= M\left(1-e^{-N/M}\right)\\
\text{share of visits from someone already seen} &= 1-\frac{1}{\text{average visits per active user}}
\end{align*}

\jpart{Daily traffic schedule}
Daypart shares from an industry article (Zane, 2 Aug 2026; no dataset cited, treated as plausible ranges):
07--11: 16\%, 11--17: 24\%, 17--23: 46\%, 23--02: 10\%, 02--07: 4\% (own assumption). About 20{,}000 visits per day.

\jpart{Calculations: two-proportion z-test}
\[
\bar p = \frac{x_1+x_2}{n_1+n_2},\qquad
SE = \sqrt{\bar p(1-\bar p)\left(\tfrac1{n_1}+\tfrac1{n_2}\right)},\qquad
z = \frac{\hat p_2-\hat p_1}{SE},\qquad p = 2\,\Phi^{c}(|z|)
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pooling: under $H_0$ both groups share one rate, so it is estimated from the two groups in the test only.
  \item z grows like $\sqrt{n}$: ten times the data gives $\sqrt{10}\approx3.16$ times the z.
  \item \code{norm.sf} vs \code{1-norm.cdf}: identical in theory; \code{sf} keeps precision in the far tail
        (at $z=9.35$, \code{1-cdf} returns 0, \code{sf} returns $4.4\times10^{-21}$).
  \item The p-value is $P(\text{data this extreme}\mid H_0)$, not $P(H_0)$.
\end{itemize}

\jpart{Calculations: Welch's t-test from totals}
Each visitor's profit is 0 or $m$ (fixed profit per order), so from totals alone:
\[
\text{mean} = m\,p,\qquad \text{variance} = m^2\,p(1-p),\qquad
t = \frac{\bar x_k-\bar x_0}{\sqrt{s_k^2/n_k + s_0^2/n_0}}
\]
Sample size for a \Rs 0.50 gap (5\% level, 80\% power):
$n \approx (1.96+0.84)^2(\sigma_0^2+\sigma_3^2)/0.5^2 \approx 34{,}000$ visitors per arm
(about 7 days of all traffic).

\jpart{Code results: light simulator (\code{light\_sim.py}, \code{class\_lib.py})}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hourly batches: visits $\sim$ Poisson(hourly $\lambda$); split $\sim$ Multinomial(visits, shares);
        orders $\sim$ Binomial(per-arm visits, true rates).
  \item Policy classes (\code{EqualSplit}) with \code{allocate}, \code{update}, \code{choice\_test};
        the class holds memory and rules, the loop is the world.
  \item Bugs fixed in \code{choice\_test}: variance used the mean instead of $m$; NumPy array has no
        \code{append}; \code{max} is a built-in, not a list method; \code{import scipy.stats as norm}
        renamed the module instead of importing the distribution.
  \item Fixed A/B test, one run: 3 days gives no winner (keeps control); 14 days picks arm 3
        ($t=4.18$, $p=2.9\times10^{-5}$).
\end{itemize}

\jpart{Calculations: A/A false-alarm rate of the fixed test}
Per comparison, $|t|>2.39$ (two-sided $0.05/3$), and only ``better than control'' counts:
$P(t>2.39)\approx0.83\%$. Three comparisons: $1-(1-0.0083)^3\approx2.5\%$ if independent; slightly
less because they share the control group, so about 2\%. For a fixed test this does not depend on
the number of days.

\jpart{Discussions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Monte Carlo (repeat with different seeds and count outcomes) is not MCMC (Markov chain Monte
        Carlo, a technique for sampling posteriors).
  \item Monte Carlo error on a rate: $\sqrt{p(1-p)/R}$, about $\pm2$ points at $R=500$ near 95\%.
  \item Regret: for every visitor, (best true profit per visitor $-$ shown arm's true profit per visitor).
        Best arm $=\max(\text{rate}\times\text{profit})$, not $\max(\text{profit})$.
  \item In A/A every arm is the best, so regret is always 0; A/A is judged by the false-alarm rate.
\end{itemize}

to the chosen arm for every remaining hour.

\jpart{Discussions: peeking}
Check the test every evening; stop at the first significant result and show that offer to everyone
for the rest of the campaign. The decision is made once. Each extra look is another chance for noise
to cross the line, so false alarms rise. Expected trade-off: lower regret, more false alarms.

\entry{20 Oct 2026: Bayesian expected-loss stopping}{code, results}

\jpart{Discussions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Instead of ``is the gap significant?'', ask ``if I pick this offer now and I'm wrong, how much
        profit per visitor do I expect to lose?''. Stop when that is below a threshold of caring.
  \item Posterior per arm: Beta$(1+\text{orders},\,1+\text{visits}-\text{orders})$. Draw $S=5000$ worlds,
        multiply by profit per order, loss $=$ row maximum $-$ value, expected loss $=$ column mean.
  \item Checked once per evening (same looks as peeking); threshold $\varepsilon=\Rs 0.01$ per visitor.
  \item Bugs fixed: Beta parameters fixed at (1,1); comparing rates instead of profit; mean over the whole
        table instead of \code{axis=0}; an attribute with the same name as a method; comparing arrays in
        \code{if}; storing a reference to \code{self.visits} instead of its sum.
  \item Sampling error of the expected loss $\approx$ spread$/\sqrt{S}\approx\Rs 0.0003$, small next to $\varepsilon$.
\end{itemize}

\entry{20 Oct 2026: Thompson sampling, richer outputs, renaming}{code, results, future scope}

\jpart{Discussions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Thompson sampling changes the traffic split during the test: each hour every offer gets a share
        equal to its probability of being the best (share of 1000 posterior worlds it wins). Bad offers are
        starved quickly; uncertain ones keep some traffic (exploration in proportion to uncertainty). It never stops.
  \item Batched version: shares recomputed once per hour, as real systems do when data arrives in batches.
  \item One campaign (normal, 3 days): offer 2 got 409 visitors instead of about 15{,}000; offer 3 about 75\%
        of all traffic.
  \item Outputs now saved per run: \code{summary\_*.csv} (winner, correct, regret, stopped, stop day) and
        \code{daily\_regret\_*.csv} (cumulative regret at the end of each day).
  \item Files renamed: \code{visit\_simulator.py}, \code{hourly\_simulator.py}, \code{monte\_carlo.py},
        \code{policies.py}, \code{stats\_tests.py}; outputs in \code{outputs/monte\_carlo}; plots numbered 01--11.
\end{itemize}

\jpart{Metrics: speed (ms per 14-day campaign)}
Fixed 5, peeking 4, Bayesian 7, Thompson 183 (1000 worlds $\times$ 4 offers drawn every hour).
